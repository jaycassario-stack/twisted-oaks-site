Locations · Philadelphia

Philadelphia wedding photographer

By Jay Cassario, Twisted Oaks Studio

I’ve been photographing Philadelphia weddings for more than 10 years, and to be honest, the city still surprises me. I live just over the bridge in South Jersey, so Philly is basically my backyard… the grand old ballrooms, the churches, City Hall, the Rodin Museum, the little side streets in Old City. A Philadelphia wedding day usually moves around a lot, from getting ready to the church to portraits somewhere in the city and then on to the reception, and that’s exactly the kind of day I love to photograph.

At a glance

  • Venues I shoot often: The Union Trust, Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben and The Vie by Cescaphe.
  • Favorite portrait spots: the Rodin Museum, City Hall, Broad Street and Old City.
  • Engagement sessions: Rittenhouse Square, Elfreth’s Alley, LOVE Park and the Delaware River waterfront.
  • Travel: no travel fee for Philadelphia weddings.
  • Collections: start at $5,200, custom tailored to each couple.

Philadelphia wedding venues I love

I’ve photographed weddings in a lot of different Philly venues over the years, but these are the ones I keep coming back to, each with a real wedding you can look through.

The Union Trust

The Union Trust on Chestnut Street is one of the most dramatic rooms in the city, with a ceremony right in the grand hall and a candlelit staircase that’s just about made for portraits. Gianna & Simon did their first look at the Rodin Museum and came back for a ceremony under a white floral chuppah and a reception that never slowed down.

Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben

The Ballroom at the Ben is gold and cream from top to bottom, with a grand staircase I always find a reason to use. Chris & Danielle had their ceremony and reception there, after a first look by the reflecting pool at the Rodin Museum.

The Vie by Cescaphe

The Vie is all about the party… a checkered dance floor, candlelit tables and a big floral wall. Branon & Celeste got married at Saint Monica in South Philly first, we did portraits at City Hall on the way, and then they spent the rest of the night on that dance floor.

Getting married in a Philadelphia church

A lot of Philly couples still get married in the church they grew up in, and some of those churches are just stunning. Saint Monica in South Philly was one of them, with tall arches and stained glass, and I love how a church ceremony gives the day a slower, quieter start before everything picks up. Branon & Celeste’s wedding is a good look at how a church ceremony, city portraits and a reception at a different venue all fit into one day.

My favorite places for wedding portraits in Philadelphia

The Rodin Museum

The Rodin Museum on the Parkway is one of my favorite spots in the whole city. You get the iron gates, the stone steps, the gardens and the reflecting pool, all within a short walk, and it works for first looks just as well as it does for portraits. Both Gianna & Simon and Chris & Danielle started their day there.

Philadelphia City Hall

City Hall is basically one giant portrait backdrop, with stone arches, doorways, staircases and courtyards in every direction. It’s right in the middle of Center City too, so it’s easy to fit in between the ceremony and the reception. Branon & Celeste and their wedding party took over a good part of it.

Old City

Old City is where Philadelphia feels the oldest… cobblestone streets, brick row homes and historic buildings like the Merchants’ Exchange, with its big stone columns. It’s a short ride from most Center City venues, and there’s a different backdrop around just about every corner, which is why I love it for portraits as much as I do for engagement sessions.

A quick note on permits: some spots in the city, like City Hall and the Rodin Museum, may require a photography permit. Getting the permit is up to you, but I’m always happy to help you figure out what you need and who to ask.

Philadelphia engagement sessions

Philly is a great city for an engagement session, especially if you love brick, cobblestone and a little bit of history. Steve & Maria did theirs around Rittenhouse Square, with brownstone steps and quiet side streets, and Rachel & Duane did theirs on Elfreth’s Alley in Old City right around Christmas, Santa hats and all (their idea, not mine). I wrote more about both in my engagement session locations guide.

There’s a lot more to work with, too. LOVE Park is a classic for a reason, the waterfront along the Delaware River gives you the Ben Franklin Bridge and some great evening light, and a coffee shop or a little restaurant, like Time on Sansom Street, makes for a relaxed start before we head outside… and to be honest, it’s usually where couples loosen up the most.

What I’ve learned photographing Philly weddings

  • Give yourself extra time to get around. Center City traffic on a Saturday is no joke, so I always build in more time between the church, portraits and the venue than it looks like you’ll need.
  • Plan how the wedding party gets around. A trolley or a shuttle keeps everyone together and on schedule, and it usually ends up being one of the more fun parts of the day.
  • Pick portrait spots close to your venue. Two great spots ten minutes apart beat five spots across the city every time.
  • Expect the light to go early. The tall buildings block the sun before it actually sets, so golden hour in the city comes a little earlier than you’d think.
  • Have a rain plan for portraits too. Not just the ceremony. Covered archways and grand lobbies can save the day.

Frequently asked questions

Do you photograph weddings in Philadelphia?

Yes. Philadelphia is just over the bridge from my studio in South Jersey, and I’ve been photographing weddings in the city for more than 10 years, at venues like The Union Trust, Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben and The Vie by Cescaphe.

Is there a travel fee for Philadelphia weddings?

No. There is no travel fee within an hour of Waterford Works, NJ, which includes Philadelphia. Venues farther away have a $200 travel fee.

How much does a Philadelphia wedding photographer cost?

At Twisted Oaks Studio, wedding collections start at $5,200, and every collection is custom tailored to the couple.

Where are the best places for wedding photos in Philadelphia?

A few of my favorites are the Rodin Museum on the Parkway, Philadelphia City Hall, Rittenhouse Square and the side streets of Old City like Elfreth’s Alley. I always try to pick spots close to your ceremony and reception so you spend more time on photos and less time in traffic.

Do I need a permit for wedding photos in Philadelphia?

Some locations, like Philadelphia City Hall and the Rodin Museum, may require a photography permit. Couples are responsible for getting any permits, but I’m always happy to help you figure out what you need and who to contact.

Do you photograph weddings in other parts of Pennsylvania?

Yes. Besides Philadelphia, I photograph weddings across Pennsylvania, from Lancaster County farmland to Bucks County estates.
