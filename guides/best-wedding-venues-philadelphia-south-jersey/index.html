The best wedding venues in Philadelphia & South Jersey

By Jay Cassario · Updated November 2026

I get asked about venues just about as often as I get asked about photography, which makes sense… I’ve spent a lot of wedding days at a lot of places around Philadelphia and South Jersey, and I see the parts most people never think about until the day of. Where the sun sits during the ceremony, how far the getting-ready rooms are from everything else, what happens if it rains. So here are the venues I keep coming back to, what I love about each one from behind the camera, and real weddings from each one you can look through.

At a glance

  • Philadelphia: The Union Trust, Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben and The Vie by Cescaphe.
  • Farms & barns in South Jersey: Bishop Farmstead and Johnson’s Locust Hall Farm.
  • The shore & country clubs: ICONA Diamond Beach, Congress Hall and Eagle Oaks Golf & Country Club.
  • Worth the drive: Renault Winery and The Woolverton Inn.
  • What I look at first: the light at ceremony time, the rain plan and how close everything is.

Philadelphia wedding venues

Philly weddings have a different energy than anywhere else I shoot. You get grand old buildings, marble and gold, and the whole city right outside the door for portraits, so even an indoor venue gives us a lot to work with.

The Union Trust

The Union Trust on Chestnut Street is basically a grand old bank turned into a ballroom, and it’s one of the most dramatic rooms I’ve ever photographed in. The ceremony happens right in the main hall, and the staircase, once it’s lined with candles, is just about the best spot in the building for a portrait. For Gianna & Simon, we did their first look and portraits at the Rodin Museum first, then came back for a ceremony under a white floral chuppah and a reception that took off the minute Gianna walked down those candlelit stairs.

Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben

The Ballroom at the Ben is all gold and cream, with a grand staircase that I’ll always find a way to use. Ceremony and reception can both happen in the building, which keeps the day simple, and like the Union Trust, the Rodin Museum is close enough for a first look and portraits before everything starts. Chris & Danielle did exactly that, and then threw one of the more fun receptions I’ve been to, Eagles jersey and all.

The Vie by Cescaphe

The Vie has a checkered dance floor, candlelit tables and a big colorful floral wall that makes a great backdrop once the party gets going. A lot of my couples who book The Vie get married at a church in the city first and do portraits around Center City on the way over, which to be honest is one of my favorite ways to spend a wedding afternoon in Philly. Branon & Celeste got married at Saint Monica in South Philly, we did portraits at City Hall, and then the night ended with umbrellas all over the dance floor.

Farm & barn wedding venues in South Jersey

This is home turf for me. Farm venues give you room to move, and the light out here in the open fields at the end of the day is hard to beat.

Bishop Farmstead, Southampton

Bishop Farmstead is one of my favorite places to photograph a wedding, period. Everyone gets ready in a separate building right on the property, the garden ceremony is set up so the sun lines up just right, there’s a big tent by the barn if it rains, and the portrait spots, from the gardens to the big red barn, are all a short walk from each other. I wrote a whole Bishop Farmstead guide if you want the details, along with weddings from Meg & Mike, Ivan & Camille, Jimmy & Kelsey and Steve & Madison.

Johnson’s Locust Hall Farm, Jobstown

Johnson’s Locust Hall Farm is a working farm in Burlington County, and it feels like it… wheat fields, a peach orchard, an old stone barn and wide-open sky in every direction. Matt & Erica got married in an open field, took a golf cart ride down the orchard lane for portraits and finished the night under the string lights in the barn. If you love golden hour, this is the place.

Shore & country club wedding venues

ICONA Diamond Beach, Wildwood

ICONA Diamond Beach puts the beach, the dunes and the palm trees right outside the ballroom, which is a pretty rare thing at the Jersey Shore. Mason & Morgan got ready there, got married at Sacred Heart in Avalon, rode a trolley back with their wedding party and still had time for portraits on the beach before the reception. The wind off the ocean can be a lot, but to be honest, a veil blowing in the wind never hurt anybody’s photos.

Congress Hall, Cape May

Congress Hall is about as classic Cape May as it gets, with that long front porch lined with rocking chairs and the beach just across the street. Mason & Morgan started their engagement session on the porch with cocktails before we walked down to the water for sunset, and it’s easy to see why so many couples want to get married there.

Eagle Oaks Golf & Country Club, Farmingdale

Eagle Oaks is a great example of why I always ask about the rain plan. Caroline & Thomas got married there in January on a cold, rainy day, and we never had to step outside… the first look and ceremony were in the bright white conservatory, the arched hallways and the covered porch gave us completely different looks for portraits, and the ballroom handled the rest.

Worth the drive

Renault Winery, Egg Harbor City

Renault is one of the oldest wineries in the country, and between the gardens, the vineyard rows, the big old trees and the open fields, there’s just about no bad direction to point a camera. Fall there is special. Vito & Kathleen had a garden ceremony under white flowers and a tented reception with a checkered dance floor, and Andrew & Janey said their vows under the wooden arch and spent golden hour out in the vines. I’ve also done an engagement session there with Morgan & Will.

The Woolverton Inn, Stockton

The Woolverton Inn is a historic stone manor up near the Delaware River, and in the spring the magnolia trees bloom pink over just about every portrait spot on the property. There are sheep in the pasture by the barn too, and they were more than happy to photobomb a few portraits with Steve & Maria.

What to look for when you tour a venue

I’m not a venue coordinator, but after this many weddings I’ve learned what makes a day easy to photograph and what makes it hard. A few things to ask about on your tour:

  • Where the sun will be at ceremony time. An outdoor ceremony facing straight into the sun is tough on everyone. Ask where the light falls for your time of year, or ask me.
  • The rain plan. Every outdoor venue should have one, and it should be somewhere you’d actually want to get married.
  • Where everyone gets ready. Getting-ready rooms on site, close to each other, mean I can go back and forth without missing anything.
  • Portrait spots within a short walk. Less time walking means more time for photos, and more time for you to actually enjoy your cocktail hour.
  • The reception lighting. String lights, chandeliers and candles look great in photos. Bright colored uplighting can be harder.
  • Sunset time on your date. Golden hour is my favorite part of the day, so I’ll always help you build your timeline around it.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best wedding venues in Philadelphia?

A few of my favorites are The Union Trust on Chestnut Street, Cescaphe Ballroom at the Ben and The Vie by Cescaphe. The Union Trust and the Ballroom at the Ben can host the ceremony and reception in one place, and the Rodin Museum is close by for a first look and portraits.

What are the best barn and farm wedding venues in South Jersey?

Bishop Farmstead in Southampton and Johnson’s Locust Hall Farm in Jobstown are two of my favorites. Both have barns for the reception, plenty of portrait spots on the property and beautiful open fields for golden hour.

What are good wedding venues at the Jersey Shore?

ICONA Diamond Beach in Wildwood has the beach and dunes right outside the ballroom, and Congress Hall in Cape May is a classic with its long front porch a short walk from the beach. For a country club near the shore, Eagle Oaks Golf & Country Club in Farmingdale has a bright conservatory and a great rain plan.

Do you photograph weddings at venues that aren’t on this list?

Yes. These are just the venues I’ve photographed the most and keep coming back to. I photograph weddings all over Philadelphia, South Jersey, the Jersey Shore and beyond, and I’m always happy to visit a new venue.
